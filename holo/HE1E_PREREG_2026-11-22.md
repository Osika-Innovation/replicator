# PREREG HE-1e — a ranking-aware rule: chamber inversion warm-starting a short optimisation

- Date: 2026-11-22
- Task: hub #63 (lane `sim`), agent `claude-replicator-paper`. Follows HE-1 (#42) and HE-1b/c (#46).
- Script: `holo/he1e_holo_equation.py`. It imports the HE-1 functions read-only and reads the HE-1 and HE-1b/c receipts read-only for the ceiling.
- Receipt: `holo/runs/he1e_receipt.json`
- Register: SIMULATED. The score is the same pressure-node proxy as HE-1. No bench claim.
- This file and the script are committed **before** the first run. HE-1d (alternating-vortex drives) is a separate study and is not touched.

## Why

HE-1c showed that chamber inversion (R1, Gerchberg–Saxton), re-derived from each chamber's own transfer, beats a stored lookup. It reaches a median 47 % of a full per-chamber optimisation.

HE-1b showed the gap has a cause. R1 matches the field site by site, but a write needs the target to be the *k quietest* sites, which is a ranking criterion.

HE-1e asks whether a short ranking-aware polish, started from R1, closes most of that gap at write time. It also asks whether R1's starting point matters, or whether any short optimisation would do as well.

## Fixed inputs

These are identical to HE-1 and HE-1b/c:
- the `cadence_closed_chamber_ops.npz` operators;
- the 13 curriculum shapes, sampled and binarised the same way;
- the per-tone write model (each tone's drive has unit norm; the predicted set is the k lowest-I sites; the score is IoU).

## Arms

There are 65 pairs: 5 wafers (0 nominal, 1–4 detuned by 0.5 %) × 13 shapes. Every arm is computed from that pair's own H_j.

| Arm | Definition |
|---|---|
| **R1** | Gerchberg–Saxton as in HE-1 (100 iterations per tone). |
| **R1+** (the rule) | One L-BFGS-B run of HE-1's R0 loss (softplus rank surrogate, temperature 0.25), started from R1's drives (real and imaginary parts as x0), maxiter **30**. |
| **Rand+** (control) | The same optimiser with the same budget (one start, maxiter 30), started from a random draw (standard normal; real parts from `default_rng(100 + j)`, imaginary parts from `default_rng(1100 + j)`, normalised per tone). |
| **Ceiling** | Full R0 (4 restarts × maxiter 300), read from the existing receipts: HE-1 `R0` for wafer 0 and HE-1b/c `R0_refit` for wafers 1–4. These were produced by the same deterministic function. |
| **Chance** | Per pair, 1,000 random per-tone unit drives (seed 7) on H_j; the 95th percentile. |

## Gates (frozen)

| Gate | Statement | Pass | Prior |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E1** (the gap closes) | The polished rule reaches most of the ceiling | Median over 65 pairs of IoU(R1+)/IoU(ceiling) ≥ 0.80 | 50% |
| **E2** (the rule's start matters) | Starting from inversion beats starting from noise at equal budget | Mean over 65 pairs of IoU(R1+) − IoU(Rand+) ≥ 0.05 | 60% |
| **E3** (it works almost everywhere) | The polished rule beats chance | R1+ > chance-95 on ≥ 59/65 pairs (90 %) | 55% |
| **E4** (polish helps) | The polish improves on the bare rule | Mean IoU(R1+) − IoU(R1) ≥ 0.10 | 80% |

Reported, not scored:
- per-shape tables (with the thin targets rod, waveguide and sphere named);
- the median IoU(Rand+)/IoU(ceiling);
- the number of loss evaluations used.

## Honesty clauses

- R1+ is no longer a zero-free-number rule. It is a rule plus a short computation at write time, done on the chamber's own calibration. A pass means "a household machine computes its drives quickly from a fresh calibration", not "the drives are a closed-form equation".
- The ceiling is the HE-1 optimiser, not a proven optimum. A ratio above 1 on a pair means the ceiling optimiser was weak there, and it will be reported as such.
- The proxy has no Gor'kov gradient terms. The 7³ grid still makes the cube and dodecahedron identical.
- No threshold changes after the first run. A rerun for a bug is disclosed with its diff.
