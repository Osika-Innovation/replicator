The Acoustic Hologram — the plate pair at the limit

Two facing plates, an object between them, and the demand that the plates record it as a hologram and play it back as a force. Derived from that demand alone: what one hologram is, what frequency is for, what the plate must and must not be, why the harmonic comb, what resolution may honestly be claimed, what the hologram records, how it is stored, and how it is scored. The architecture that cannot be improved further — and the prototype that is the same architecture with a smaller brain.

0 · The rule of this document

Start from the demand, not from parts: two opposing circular plates, any object between them, a perfect holographic image of it, storable on an analog holographic medium, replayable as a force. Every design statement below is either forced by that demand plus a law, or is marked as a choice. "Cannot be improved further" means: each element sits against a bound (diffraction, absorption, linearity, reciprocity, mode count) rather than against a budget. Where a bound is reached the paper says which; where the design is short of it the paper says by how much and why.

1 · What one hologram is

A monochromatic wave field in a source-free region is completely determined by its complex value — amplitude and phase — on a closed surface around it (Kirchhoff–Helmholtz; the Rayleigh integrals for a plane). The plate pair is that surface to within an equatorial band (§7). So a hologram of the object at frequency f is the complex pressure on the two plates at f, and nothing more is needed to reconstruct the whole 3-D field between them to the diffraction limit. DERIVED (textbook; optical holography is the measured instance).

Sound records phase directly. Optics must interfere the object wave with a reference beam to make phase visible; a piezo element or microphone reads complex pressure as such. Acoustic hologram acquisition is therefore sampling, not interferometry — the "phase-kept scan is hologram acquisition" identity of R12 stated at the element. MEASURED (near-field acoustic holography, Maynard/Williams lineage).

Sample budget. A plate of area A radiating at wavelength λ needs one complex sample per (λ/2)² — the space–bandwidth product NSBP = 4A/λ². This is the ceiling on every channel-count question in this paper.

2 · What frequency is for — voxel size, and the price of it

Because one frequency already carries the whole geometry (§1), frequency buys exactly one thing for the image: voxel size, λ/2 in every direction once angular coverage is complete. Everything else frequency does is either a cost (absorption) or a bonus (§5). T60 is passive cavity storage, not actuator latency. A chamber's 20–50 ms reverberation time at 100–200 kHz is the lifetime over which the air volume stores standing-wave energy (Qcav = πfT60/ln 103 ~ 103–104), not a mechanical update frame period. Actuator state changes are bounded by the piezoelectric crystal's acoustic transit inertia τinertia = L/vD ~ 0.1–0.3 µs (§4.1), five orders of magnitude faster than T60. DERIVED

What the closed chamber does and does not buy. Confinement does not defeat absorption: loss is per metre travelled, and in a reverberant box what matters is α·c in dB per second. Above ~300 kHz the field is one-pass whether or not the box is closed. What the chamber does buy: (i) reverberation up to ~200 kHz — the mixing that makes a few channels worth thousands (§3); (ii) atmosphere control — the 40 kHz–1 MHz loss is dominated by O₂/N₂ molecular relaxation (humidity-dependent) plus classical ∝ f² loss, so humidity is a knob, and a monatomic fill (argon: no molecular relaxation) drops 200 kHz–1 MHz loss several-fold; (iii) designable nonlinearity — shock distance ∝ 1/(f·p₀) is centimetres at 200 kHz and ~150 dB, so parametric and harmonic operation are features, not confounds. Plan A stays ordinary air (R11); argon fill is the named roadmap rung that opens 500 kHz–1 MHz, and that rung requires the pixel-dense aperture of §10 because a one-pass field has no reverberation for a few-channel drive to exploit. DERIVED (R11 confirmed; the argon rung is this paper's addition to R11's medium shelf).

Design point. Top of the plan-A band = 200 kHz (0.85 mm voxel; single-digit dB per pass over a 0.3–0.5 m path; reverberation still usable; thin-plate physics still valid at 1 mm). R11's 300 kHz envelope stands as the ceiling; 200 kHz is where every element is still on the good side of its bound at once.

3 · The plate as a MIMO channel — what "how many speakers" really asks

Drop "how many transducers" as the question. The plate (plus chamber) is a linear channel from K drives to pressure at every point of the working volume: a transfer matrix H(f). Measure it once (microphone/vibrometer scan — the twin's calibration), and the hologram problem is: find drive signals whose image under H is the target field. The honest capacity questions are properties of H's singular-value spectrum:

  • Effective rank Neff — singular values above the noise/actuator floor: the number of genuinely different fields the aperture can make. Ceiling: NSBP of §1.
  • Capacity Σ log₂(1 + SNR·σᵢ²) if one number is wanted.

Every design lever below — symmetry, thickness, Q, channel count, the chamber — is a lever on that spectrum. This is the same MIMO/transmission- matrix idea that a multimode fibre (§8) and a Wi-Fi array use. DERIVED (time-reversal and transmission- matrix art MEASURED: Fink lineage; "acoustic touch" glass localises a fingertip anywhere with 2–4 piezos by plate reverberation — the reciprocal of what we build).

3.1 The counting rule

Per frequency-resolution slot Δf (the modal or reverberant linewidth) the aperture can command min(K, M) independent patterns, where M is the number of modes overlapping that slot. Summed over the band B:

N_eff ≈ Σ_slots min(K, M)  ≈  min( K · B/Δf ,  N_modes(B) )

Thin-plate modal density is n(f) = A√3 / (h·cL′) (cL′ the plate longitudinal speed), constant in frequency. Worked: Ø400 borosilicate 1 mm → ~40 modes/kHz → ~8 × 10³ modes below 200 kHz; 2 mm Al 5052 → ~20 modes/kHz (the registered Chladni bench object's "~2,100 modes in band" is this number over its band). On a plate alone with resolved modes, Neff ≈ the number of radiating modes in band, almost independent of channel count — three drives and six drives make the same ~10³–10⁴ patterns. Channels multiply the count only where modes overlap within a slot: the damped bench plate, and above all the reverberant chamber. 3.2 Monochromatic vs broadband — the caveat that keeps this honest

At one frequency and one instant, K drives are K complex weights: a K-element array, however rich the plate. The thousands above are frequency-multiplexed. For a pressure image they are sequential snapshots. For a force hologram they are simultaneous, because radiation force is quadratic and time-averaged: cross terms between different frequencies average out (beat ≫ mechanical bandwidth), so sub-holograms on different lines add linearly in force space. Contrast of a superposition falls as ~1/√N; force per site as 1/N (§9). And the reachable set is the span of H's columns — a target outside it is simply not makeable with that aperture; the compiler (RSW-1) reports reachability rather than pretending.

4 · What the plate must be — and how little that is

The hologram lives in the drive signals; the plate is a mixing medium. It has to satisfy four things and no fifth:

  1. Radiate. A bending plate radiates efficiently only above its coincidence frequency fc = cair²/(1.8·cL′·h). 1 mm glass: ~12 kHz; 2 mm Al: ~6 kHz — both far below the band. Above fc bending waves are supersonic and each radiates a plane wave at sin θ = cair/cB, cB ∝ √f. Consequence: no horn is required for impedance matching in the working band — the plate is already an efficient radiator; a thickness/stiffness gradient is a built-in lens (angle follows local cB) at zero depth. R3's horn + gyroid front end solves a below-coincidence and viscothermal problem the thin plate does not have at 40–200 kHz; it remains correct engineering where a design drives a thick plate or needs the gyroid's EM GRIN reading (§11, amendment a). DERIVED
  2. Be thin and low-loss. ~1 mm keeps thin-plate physics valid to ~400 kHz (bending λ ≥ ~5h), doubles modal density against 2 mm, keeps fc low. Borosilicate glass: internal Q in the thousands, isotropic, transparent (the optical addressing of §10 comes through it); aluminium is the metal alternative. Polymers are excluded — internal loss eats the reverberation the count in §3 relies on. Q is then set, per R6: session-calibrated bench → damp to Q ≈ 300 (ABH rim); the continuously self-calibrating machine keeps the high-Q bins. DERIVED
  3. Share no symmetry with its drives. The pathology is the cymatics default — a symmetric disc driven at its centre reaches only the m = 0 Bessel family. Three drives at 120° with independent signals already break the symmetry as an excitation (they reach m ≡ 0, ±1 mod 3), and are what R4 needs for OAM m = ±1 by phasing; what they can never reach is the sine-class m = 3k modes whose nodal lines pass through all three points (~1/6 of the spectrum), and doublet degeneracy costs one channel per slot. The aperiodic diffuse-spectrum face of R4 lifts the remaining degeneracy — that is exactly what "spectral diffuseness" buys in H-language: a well-conditioned column set. Cheap insurance beyond it: drives at three radii, an off-centre bore or D-cut. Never at the centre; not at a clamped rim. DERIVED
  4. Free-ish edge. Point/soft support (the transducers can be the supports, as a Chladni driver post is); clamping kills rim modes and adds loss.
Everything else is optional bias. Graded thickness, gyroid, micro-horn face, cochlear grading (§5.3) can improve conditioning or add a fixed lens; none is a precondition. The plate need not be anything specific — it needs to not be symmetric, thick, lossy, or clamped.

4.1 The transducer — crystal transit inertia, two-terminal HAPTIC self-sensing, and dispersive SIMO

Every count in §3 is a time–bandwidth product. A bolt-clamped Langevin transducer is a resonant dumbbell whose steel/aluminium end masses give it a ~1–2 kHz usable bandwidth and ~4.5 ms ring-down at Q ≈ 500; three of them give ~10–20 patterns — three speakers on a Chladni plate — and bolt preloading also exceeds thin glass's tensile strength. Langevin cartridges are valid for single-frequency CW power demonstrations; they are the wrong element for a broadband, sub-microsecond holographic loop. DERIVED

The optimal broadband element for Rung 1 is therefore a thin unbacked or composite piezo disc/annulus bonded below its thickness mode onto the 1–1.5 mm supersonic plate (sizing rule: patch width ≤ λB/2, so ≤ 5 mm discs or concentric annuli avoid flexural nulls up to 200 kHz, optionally mated to a replaceable thin-walled chaotic scatterer faceplate with a coaxial Ø12 mm bore). The bandwidth limit now sits in electrical headroom rather than steel end masses.

Simultaneous two-terminal HAPTIC self-sensing (zero TX/RX dead time). Treat the piezoelectric transducer from first principles as a two-port electromechanical state whose crystal coordinate x(t) (expanded vs. contracted) and instantaneous velocity ẋ(t) obey the coupled Butterworth–Van Dyke equations: DERIVED

m·ẍ + c·ẋ + k·x = Θ·V(t) + F_echo(t)
I(t) = C₀·dV/dt + Θ·ẋ(t)   [charge form: q(t) = C₀·V(t) + Θ·x(t)]

Wiring a differential full H-bridge (+Vbus expand, −Vbus contract, 0 V active brake, Hi-Z float) across both terminals (V+, V−) of a collocated RP2350B head (< 2 pF trace capacitance vs. C₀ ≈ 171 pF) turns every drive pin into a continuous sensor without TX/RX dead time. On multi-tone harmonic combs, bare zero-crossing timing fails: airborne echoes from a 2 mm droplet arrive at −60 to −80 dB below the drive current, PZT-8 dielectric capacitance C₀(T) drifts by ~0.3%/°C (−50 dB, 10–30× larger than the scatter echo), and a single comparator crossing captures the dominant tone within < 0.2 ns while failing to invert 5 comb harmonics (10 I/Q unknowns). Adding 1 fast comparator plus a 2R/1C feedback loop across 2 PIO pins against an uncoupled C₀ reference piezo runs an ADC-free 75 MSa/s 1-bit sigma-delta (ΣΔ) bridge (OSR = 250 over 150 kHz → 12–16 ENOB / 74–96 dB linear dynamic range) paired with multiplier-free XOR + popcount lock-in across 1 12-pin drive SM and 1–2 banked ΣΔ SMs out of 12 PIO state machines on a shared XIN + SYNC clock/phase bus (6.67 ns / ~0.1°). That subtracts C₀·dV/dt and local efference copies directly in hardware to expose motional velocity ẋ(t) and echo force F_echo(t) continuously during TX (MEASURED self-sensing bridge art: Dosch, Inman & Garcia 1992, 10.1177/1045389X9200300109; bench coupon K-HAPTIC-PZT; Compute, sensing and control). Continuous tracking keeps high-Q chamber modes locked across 1 °C thermal drift; discrete chirped TX/RX gates remain optional for baseline empty-chamber H mapping rather than a frame-rate bottleneck.

4.2 Coupled-cavity harmony and minimum holding power

Holding a stationary mass against gravity performs zero mechanical work (F·dz = 0 when ż = 0). Because the ~10⁴ air-to-solid impedance ratio (415 Rayl vs. ~46 MRayl for steel) reflects 99.996% at a dense solid surface, airborne acoustic levitation of heavy metals such as steel (~7.85 g/cm³) up to iridium (22.6 g/cm³; MEASURED single-axis resonant art: Xie et al. 2006) does not resonate inside the steel bulk; instead it builds a high reactive standing wave inside the coupled chamber + sphere air cavity. Locking the HAPTIC autodyne/PLL bridge onto the object-shifted coupled eigen-chord sustains kPa trapping pressure reactively through Qcav and throttles steady-state drive down to pure cavity dissipation once ẋ(t) matches the target chord (residual → 0): P_hold = ω·E_stored / Q_cav DERIVED. Extending dense-metal holding from 1-D resonant axes to 3-D multi-tone dispersive chords in our chamber remains OPEN.

5 · The chord — one comb, three octaves

5.1 What octaves buy for the image (a pyramid, not information)

With full angular coverage a single frequency fills the whole k-space ball of radius 2k; lower frequencies add no geometry the top one lacks. What a log-spaced ladder (constant fractional bandwidth per band — the cochlea's sampling) does add: coarse-to-fine reconstruction and robustness where the top octave is lost to absorption or occlusion; penetration (low bands pass what high bands only see the surface of); speckle decorrelation; and spectral colour by size class — an object rings at the band whose wavelength matches it, so octave n images structure of scale ~λn/2. DERIVED

5.2 Why a harmonic comb — the nonlinearity closes it

At force-grade SPL air is nonlinear and generates 2f, 3f and every sum/difference of what is driven. An arbitrary carrier set sprays intermodulation everywhere; a harmonic comb f₀, 2f₀, 3f₀ … is closed under mixing (nf₀ ± mf₀ stays on the comb). The field stays "in tune": spectrally sparse, coherently sampleable, harmonic imaging (drive f, listen 2f — R12's "few ×" rung) built in. A phase-locked comb is a periodic pulse train in time — the broadband time-reversal pulse and the harmonic chord are one object seen on two axes. Radiation-force cross terms between comb lines land at multiples of f₀, never at DC, so force sub-holograms on different lines still add cleanly (§3.2). Pure octaves alone are not closed (2+4=6), so the comb is the primitive and octaves are its grouping into scale classes. DERIVED (parametric-array and harmonic-imaging art MEASURED)

Choice: f₀ ≈ 25 kHz, harmonics 1–8 (25–200 kHz; 1–12 fills R11's 300 kHz envelope), grouped as three octaves 25/50/100→200 kHz = scale classes 7 → 3.4 → 1.7 → 0.85 mm.

5.3 On the plate, an octave is √2 — and a graded plate is a cochlea

Bending waves are dispersive: λB ∝ 1/√f, and the radiation angle moves by the same √2 per octave — frequency is a spatial coordinate on the plate. A plate with a graded thickness/stiffness localises each frequency to a place (tonotopy); an exponential gradient gives a log map, one octave per unit length. This is the disciplined form of the graded-structure idea (R3's "3-D rainbow library" moved into the plane): a reverse cochlea in which a few broadband channels address place by pitch, and each comb line owns a ring. Optional (§4), coherent with the comb, and it makes the plate's frequency→angle steering designable rather than incidental. DERIVED (cochlear tonotopy MEASURED; in-plate use design)

6 · Resolution, honestly — what bandwidth does and does not buy

The field is linear. Every launched component is spatially band-limited to its own 2k; a sum of band-limited patterns is band-limited to the largest. Chirping 40–200 kHz does not sharpen the pressure image below λmin/2 — the other 159 kHz add depth resolution c/2B ≈ 1 mm (a single tone has almost none along the axis without full angular coverage), speckle averaging, and the pattern count of §3. That is worth having; it is not "arbitrarily fine". DERIVED

7 · What the hologram records — not monochrome, but two-polarisation

Sound scatters from two contrasts, with different angular signatures (first-order Born: f(θ) ∝ k²[γκ − γρ cos θ]):

  • compressibility (stiffness) contrast → a monopole, isotropic;
  • density contrast → a dipole, ∝ cos θ — forward and back with opposite sign, nothing sideways.

Forward scatter (bottom plate → object → top plate) reads γκ − γρ; back-scatter to the same plate reads γκ + γρ. Two equations, two unknowns: the two-plate geometry is what makes stiffness and density separable at one frequency — the acoustic analogue of a polarimetric image (one wavelength, two "polarisations" split by viewing direction), and the same Δκ/Δρ vector R12 names as the sees-as-it-holds identity, now with its angular mechanism stated. In storage that is two pages per comb line (top view, bottom view). DERIVED (Born scattering; USCT κ/ρ separation MEASURED art in fluid coupling)

Two honest limits. (i) In air, solids saturate both contrasts (impedance ratio ~10⁴): steel and wood scatter as the same rigid surface — shape beautifully, material barely; moderate contrasts (foams, powders, liquids, gels) do separate. R12's "silhouettes + shadows + multi-bounce" stands. (ii) Solids carry shear and everything has loss, so the full state is κ, ρ, shear modulus, damping — one frequency and two angles cannot get all of it. This is where the comb becomes colour: a held part is a resonator and its ring-down spectrum (natural frequencies ~√(E/ρ)/L, damping) is a material signature even when its scattering strength is saturated — the chord list of Scan §3. Geometry from the monochrome hologram; material from the angular split where contrast is moderate, and from the spectrum where it is not. Material identification of rigid parts by ring-down in this chamber: OPEN.

The missing wedge. Two plates cover both polar hemispheres; what they cannot see is the equatorial band — the side view. Perfect (λ/2)³ voxels need the full k-sphere; the six side panels are what fill it (R8's "high-angle coverage"; the photonic panel note of 2026-08-10). Stated as architecture: the plates are the hologram; the panels close the k-sphere. The κ/ρ separation above does not need the panels — the θ = 0, π extremes suffice. DERIVED

8 · Storage and the optical interface — light carries the picture

An analog holographic medium stores the interference of two coherent optical beams as a phase (index) grating and re-emits the object wave when re-illuminated by the reference. Our acoustic hologram is a 2-D complex map on each plate; the chain that keeps it analog end to end:

  1. Fibre carries a picture, not a signal. Single-mode fibre carries one spatial mode — using it means digitising and rebuilding with a projector, which is the electronic route. Multimode fibre carries ≈(π·d·NA/λ)²/4 spatial modes: 400 µm core ≈ 7 × 10⁴, 1 mm core ≈ 4 × 10⁵ at visible — one fat fibre holds a 200 kHz plate (1.8 × 10⁵ pixels); a coherent bundle carries it unscrambled at lower count. Natural spine: one fibre per plate, or one per octave ring (§5.3). DERIVED
  2. Record through the scramble. A multimode fibre scrambles the picture by a fixed linear transform. Photorefractive holography is a phase-conjugate mirror: send the plate-side light backwards through the fibre into the medium, it stores the scrambled version; play it back into the same fibre and the fibre unscrambles it onto the plate. Fibre distortion cancels; the medium holds "the hologram as seen through that fibre" — the optical twin of "calibrate H once". MEASURED (image transmission through multimode fibre by phase conjugation, 1980s photorefractive art) ; in-machine OPEN
  3. Both directions on one fibre. Playback: medium → fibre → light picture on the plate → the addressed aperture of §10 (electric carrier does the work). Scan: plate as receiver → piezo strain read optically (photoelastic/interferometric readout in the same film) → light picture back down the fibre → interferes with the reference → recorded. Storage output is transducer input; transducer output is storage input. No digitisation anywhere in the loop. Reciprocity again. OPEN (each element measured art; the composed loop is design)

Phase. A hologram is amplitude and phase; phase carries the geometry, amplitude can be dropped (kinoform) or re-encoded as phase (detour phase) at modest fidelity cost — so a phase medium fits, and the quadrature-gated aperture of §10 supplies full complex control regardless. Capacity: Bragg-multiplexed thick media hold ~10¹²–10¹³ bits/cm³ against ~3 × 10⁶ bits per 200 kHz frame — ~10⁵–10⁶ acoustic frames per cm³. Which medium (photorefractive vs the phase-change store) is owned by Memory & Compute; this paper fixes only the interface: optical, bidirectional, phase-conjugate, through multimode fibre. DERIVED

Risk register. Multimode transmission matrices drift with bending and temperature; phase-conjugate recording cancels only what was there at record time. Mitigation: short rigid thermally-stable runs and continuous re-calibration by reciprocity (probe back through, re-record) — the same drift problem the acoustic H has (R6), solved the same way. Coherence: one laser feeds reference and object arms; path differences of metres need only a decent single-frequency source. Wavelength: photorefractives and a-Si photoconductors both prefer visible; fibre loss at visible over metres is negligible.

11 · Proposed rulings (candidate R17) and gates

For adoption; each is stated so it can be refused on its own.

  1. Horn optional above coincidence. The primary acoustic aperture is a thin (≈1 mm) low-loss plate driven above fc; R3's horn + graded gyroid front end is retained as a plan for thick-plate or sub-coincidence designs and for its EM GRIN duty, not as a precondition of the acoustic hologram. Amends R3 letter; keeps its rationale.
  2. Crystal-inertia-limited broadband elements, not Langevin dumbbells, for the hologram. Thin (0.5–1.5 mm) bare/composite piezos sized below the λB null (τinertia = L/vD ≈ 90–285 ns) driven across both terminals by differential HAPTIC H-bridges with 10–50× electrical headroom; zero TX/RX switching dead time replaces mandatory 20 ms frame gating. Heavy Q ≈ 500 Langevin slugs only for narrowband CW power comparisons.
  3. Symmetric ring allowed, drive supplies asymmetry, face supplies diffuseness. Confirms R4's 3-PZT OAM ring (or asymmetric 3-element diffuse port layout / hybrid chaotic scatterer faceplate); forbids centre drive and clamped rims.
  4. Design top of plan-A band = 200 kHz within R11's 300 kHz envelope; argon fill added to R11's medium shelf as the named rung to 1 MHz, tied to rung 2.
  5. One comb, three octaves. f₀ ≈ 25 kHz harmonic comb; octaves as scale classes; force sub-holograms add per comb line.
  6. Resolution register: pressure λ/2, force pixel λ/4, depth c/2B, placement λ/100 (R12 unchanged; adds the force-pixel row).
  7. Two-polarisation, not monochrome: κ/ρ separation by forward/back angle at one frequency; panels close the k-sphere (adds mechanism to R12's Δκ/Δρ identity and to R8's high-angle duty).
